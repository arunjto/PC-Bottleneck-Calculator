## The State of the GPU Market in 2026

Current NVIDIA Blackwell and AMD RDNA 4 cards span entry-level through enthusiast price tiers. The right choice depends on the exact games and applications you use, your display, local price, power supply, case clearance, and tolerance for upscaling or frame generation.

Both manufacturers have focused on **ray tracing**, **AI-assisted upscaling and frame generation**, and **power efficiency**. Gains vary significantly by game, settings, and whether generated frames are included, so compare like-for-like benchmarks for your own workload.

This guide groups several current models by launch-price tier and intended use. It is a specification-based shortlist, not a laboratory performance ranking. Verify current street price and independent benchmarks for your exact workload before buying.

> **Note:** All pricing reflects MSRP at launch. Street prices may vary depending on availability and regional markets.

## Budget Tier: Options Around $300

### NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5060

The **RTX 5060** is a Blackwell option with 8GB of GDDR7 memory and a $299 launch MSRP. NVIDIA positions it primarily for 1080p gaming; results at any resolution vary by title and settings. Key specifications include:

- **CUDA Cores:** 3,840
- **Memory:** 8GB GDDR7
- **Total Graphics Power:** 145W
- **MSRP:** $299

The RTX 5060 supports NVIDIA's current DLSS feature set, but performance and image quality depend on each game's implementation. Treat it primarily as a 1080p option and check independent tests for the titles you play.

### AMD Radeon RX 9060 XT 8GB

AMD's **RX 9060 XT 8GB** is an RDNA 4 alternative at the same launch MSRP as the RTX 5060:

- **Stream Processors:** 2,048
- **Memory:** 8GB GDDR6
- **TDP:** 150W
- **MSRP:** $299

The 8GB model targets 1080p gaming. A 16GB RX 9060 XT is also available for workloads that benefit from more VRAM, but board power and pricing differ.

## Mid-Range: Options Between $400–$600

### NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070

The **RTX 5070** is a 1440p-focused option with 12GB of GDDR7. NVIDIA's promotional comparisons can include Multi Frame Generation, which is not equivalent to native rendered performance, so do not treat it as an RTX 4090 replacement. Key specifications are:

- **CUDA Cores:** 6,144
- **Memory:** 12GB GDDR7
- **TDP:** 250W
- **MSRP:** $549

For 1440p gaming, the RTX 5070 can be a strong option when its feature set suits your games. Ray-traced results vary widely, especially at maximum settings.

### AMD Radeon RX 9070

AMD's **RX 9070** provides 16GB of GDDR6 memory and a 256-bit interface at a $549 launch MSRP:

- **Stream Processors:** 3,584
- **Memory:** 16GB GDDR6
- **Typical Board Power:** 220W
- **MSRP:** $549

Its 16GB capacity may help with large texture packs and some creator workloads. Software support and application-specific acceleration should also be checked before choosing it for productivity.

## Higher-Performance Options: $599–$1000

### NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5080

The **RTX 5080** is a 16GB Blackwell card with a $999 launch MSRP. NVIDIA positions it for high-resolution gaming and creator workloads, but value depends on local price and application-specific benchmarks:

- **CUDA Cores:** 10,752
- **Memory:** 16GB GDDR7
- **TDP:** 360W
- **MSRP:** $999

The RTX 5080 targets high-setting 4K gaming, but frame rate depends on the title, ray-tracing level, and whether upscaling or generated frames are enabled.

### AMD Radeon RX 9070 XT

AMD's **RX 9070 XT** is the faster RDNA 4 option in this guide and launched below the RTX 5080's price tier:

- **Stream Processors:** 4,096
- **Memory:** 16GB GDDR6
- **Typical Board Power:** 304W
- **MSRP:** $599

The RX 9070 XT is positioned for high-refresh 1440p and 4K gaming. Compare current independent benchmarks because ray tracing, upscaling, and raster results vary by title.

## Enthusiast Tier

### NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090

The **RTX 5090** has the highest published specifications among the GeForce models in this shortlist. It targets demanding high-resolution and creator workloads, but no fixed frame-rate claim applies across all games:

- **CUDA Cores:** 21,760
- **Memory:** 32GB GDDR7
- **TDP:** 575W
- **MSRP:** $1,999

The RTX 5090 is the highest-spec GeForce card in this list. Its 575W total graphics power calls for NVIDIA's recommended 1000W system power and careful attention to case clearance, cabling, and airflow.

## How to Choose the Right GPU

Start with these factors:

1. **Your target resolution** — 1080p, 1440p, or 4K
2. **Your games and applications** — Compare independent tests using similar settings, patches, and upscaling modes
3. **Your total budget** — Include the power supply, case, cooling, display, and any platform upgrade needed
4. **Physical and electrical compatibility** — Check dimensions, connectors, recommended system power, and the PSU maker's cable instructions
5. **Current value and support** — Compare street price, warranty, return terms, drivers, and software features

There is no universal percentage of a PC budget that should go to the GPU. Allocate more for GPU-heavy workloads and less when the CPU, memory capacity, display, storage, or specialist software matters more. Use the calculator only as an initial score comparison, then validate the proposed pairing with relevant benchmarks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 8GB of VRAM enough in 2026?

There is no universal VRAM requirement by resolution. Usage depends on the game, texture setting, ray tracing, mods, rendering mode, and background applications. Check recent tests for the titles you play; if a test shows memory-related stutter or forced texture reductions, compare higher-capacity options within your budget. Our [VRAM requirements guide](/en/blog/how-much-vram-do-you-need-for-gaming) provides a transparent capacity-planning worksheet for 1080p, 1440p and 4K.

### Should I buy NVIDIA or AMD?

Both have worthwhile options. NVIDIA emphasizes ray tracing, DLSS, and its creator software stack; AMD's competing cards may offer more memory at some price points. Compare the exact games and applications you use rather than choosing by brand alone.

### When is the best time to buy a GPU?

There is no reliable universal buying window. Compare the current street price with launch MSRP, check retailer return terms, and look at recent benchmarks because driver updates and competing launches can change value.
